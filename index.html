the book

December 23, 1982

Dear Anil,

I wish I had time to handwrite this so that it would be more personal, but this year has been so rushed, that it is impossible. So I will go with modern technology and opt for a higher wpm.

I'm glad you are interested in the Indian cooking and in your heritage. For some reason, it seems to be in the nature of human beings to take pride in their background. There are times when that has turned me off - so to speak. You know the types I mean. "Oh, we are English so we are entitled to rule the world." "Or we are terrific - all those foreigners are stupid and dirty." I never see that sort of thing in you though and I am glad. Your interest in your background is a healthy one and a happy one and while I confess to being an awful mother - a careless mother - a mother who just did things off the cuff - but I am so pleased that you turned out to be compassionate. There were times when I wondered about you - you were terrible to your sisters. But you turned out OK.

So, it is with pleasure that I pass on to you our family recipes.

Love,

why now

November 7, 2026

I’ve always loved my grandmother’s work (her writings, drawings, paintings, recipes, and the genuine sense of self that she brought to all of it…) but I’ve rarely shared it with others. I arrived 66 years late to her story, and while I treasure it, I don’t believe that it is mine to tell.

This book, for example, was one of her famously perfect and personal Christmas gifts – made for my favorite (and only) uncle, the sweet baby boy of the family, anil.

anil was the one who taught me to spell my first name in lower-case because we as humans are just nouns. There is absolutely nothing proper about us.

I shared the book for the first time this summer with my dear friend John. It only occurred to me to share it because he asked for exactly what it is: lentils and simple Indian home cooking and uncomplicated ingredients… the kind of thing that someone could make in, say, 1950s Indiana with just one dollar in their pocket.

John is a retired English professor and immediately told me that I had to publish it. He said that it was food that needed to be eaten and stories that need to be heard and, most of all, that now was the time.

So I asked anil for his permission, and then I googled how to publish a cookbook. What I found is that you have to have “an audience.” That’s all that this account is. Please follow, share, enjoy. Show your support… and hopefully, someday, I’ll be able to send you a copy in the mail along with an invitation to a very big family dinner party.

I love this book. I love my family. I love everything that they have loved and fought for and still stand for in the minds of everyone who really knew them. The idea that love can be controversial in this world is still crazy to me, so today, it is with pleasure that I share my family’s story.

Thank you,

“So, it is with pleasure that I pass on to you our family recipes.” — betty, december 23, 1982

a look inside

FRIED LENTILS

NOTE:  I always have a fondness for these lentils.  Our first Christmas together was as poor in terms of wealth as one could have.  I had one dollar.  With my one dollar, I bought your father a bottle of cardamons.

We lived in a raunchy part of town in Fort Wayne - the part reserved for "blacks".  We just could find a place anywhere else.  They had always just rented the place.  One man was a little more frank.  He took me aside and said he didn't like my husband's attitude.  He considered him to be too proud.

Our apartment was not bad though.  It was on the second floor.  There was a large kitchen, a smaller living room and a large bedroom.  The furniture wasn't elegant but it wasn't crummy either.  We had a small tree.  I don't remember what we bought that with.  Your father wasn't working at the time since he had just graduated and we were about to head to Iowa.  Our Christmas dinner was lentils.  We made sandwiches out of them - put them between toasted bread.  No turkey could have tasted better.

Looking back, I think that was one of the most precious Christmas' we ever had.  Everything seemed very bleak.  At the time, I was still involved with the church and we went to a midnight eve service at a Lutheran church.  It was really beautiful - they had decorated with pine and red ribbons and there were candles everywhere - and a creche.  The music was well done - really superb.  And we went back to our little apartment in the "colored" part of town feeling happy in spite of all the problems we knew we faced.

Put lentils in the skillet and fry in liberal amount of butter.  Add salt and red pepper.  Cover with water - just enough to cover them.  Cook slowly without a lid until the water evaporates.  They should be done by that time.  Taste - if they need butter, add some until they taste right.  I like to cut raw onions on them and eat them with vinegar sometimes.
